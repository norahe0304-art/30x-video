# Enterprise Design Archetypes

Use design references to sharpen judgment, not to clone websites. `awesome-design-md` is a semantic library, not a template source. Start with `BrandTruth`, then infer the archetype that best explains the brand's typography, surface depth, density, and motion temperament.

## Inference Rules

1. **Brand reality beats archetype.** If screenshots or video disagree with the inferred archetype, trust the evidence.
2. **One primary, one secondary max.** More than two archetypes means the model is hedging.
3. **Do not copy branded specifics.** Reuse logic, not Stripe purple or Linear's exact layout.
4. **Screenshots can infer density; video can infer motion temperament.** If video is missing, downgrade motion ambition.
5. **Enterprise trust beats spectacle.** For AI infra, devtool, security, fintech, and data products, credibility outranks ornament.

## Archetypes

### 1. Financial Precision
- **Reference signal:** Stripe-class design analysis; premium finance and payments products.
- **Visual temperament:** white canvas, deep navy anchors, selective color energy, controlled surfaces, refined depth, high polish without glow spam.
- **Typography temperament:** light-to-regular display weights, sharp hierarchy, disciplined tracking, tabular numerals for proof.
- **Density / radius / spacing:** low-to-medium density, conservative radius, clean segmentation, measured whitespace, strong panel edges.
- **Motion temperament:** deliberate and luxurious; gliding reveals, confident elevation, no playful bounce.
- **Use for:** fintech, payments, enterprise revenue products, trust-first launches.

### 2. System Clarity
- **Reference signal:** Linear-class product systems; execution-focused B2B tools.
- **Visual temperament:** dark product surfaces, cool neutrals, indigo-violet restraint, crisp interface rhythm, operational calm.
- **Typography temperament:** utilitarian but elegant; headings feel precise, body text never ornamental, utility labels stay tight and disciplined.
- **Density / radius / spacing:** medium-to-high information density, strong alignment, narrow waste, disciplined grids, compact but never cramped.
- **Motion temperament:** deliberate and low-amplitude; transitions suggest responsiveness, not theater.
- **Use for:** productivity tools, PM systems, ops software, structured B2B products.

### 3. Editorial Minimalism
- **Reference signal:** Vercel-class presentation; content-led technology brands.
- **Visual temperament:** monochrome precision, black/white or near-white surfaces, negative space, strong verbal cadence, restrained surfaces.
- **Typography temperament:** typography carries the emotion; weight and scale do the heavy lifting, mono support only where system labels matter.
- **Density / radius / spacing:** sparse frames, large breathing zones, low chrome, high confidence, small radius with crisp edges.
- **Motion temperament:** crisp and quiet; subtle reveals, slow camera logic, text and layout shifts over effect-heavy animation.
- **Use for:** platform launches, brand-led product announcements, products with strong positioning copy.

### 4. Infra Authority
- **Reference signal:** enterprise AI infra, security, developer platform, data systems.
- **Visual temperament:** command-center seriousness, high signal-to-noise ratio, proof over spectacle, product surface is the hero.
- **Typography temperament:** direct, technical, readable at a glance; labels and metrics matter, tabular numerals should carry proof.
- **Density / radius / spacing:** denser UI, modular panes, more evidence per frame, no decorative dead weight, explicit separation between proof groups.
- **Motion temperament:** measured and verifiable; use evidence, dashboards, timelines, logs, graphs, or operator gestures, then hold long enough to read.
- **Use for:** AI infrastructure, security, observability, developer tools, data platforms.

### 5. Productive Warmth
- **Reference signal:** collaboration and productivity brands that still need enterprise trust.
- **Visual temperament:** humane, approachable, but still ordered and credible; warmth comes from surface tone, not decoration.
- **Typography temperament:** softer but not whimsical; warmth through tone, not cute styling or novelty type.
- **Density / radius / spacing:** medium density, approachable spacing, gentle surface contrast, slightly softer shells than infra or systems products.
- **Motion temperament:** smoother, more inviting, but still purposeful; no cartoon energy, no consumer-app hyperactivity.
- **Use for:** collaboration tools, knowledge products, workflow software, prosumer-to-enterprise brands.

## Archetype Application

When the archetype is chosen, apply it to:

- **Typography:** font choice, weight range, tracking, headline scale
- **Surface logic:** card depth, border behavior, shadow tone, panel layering
- **Density:** how much UI can fit in one frame before trust degrades
- **Motion:** spring stiffness, hold times, transition restraint, reveal style
- **Proof style:** dashboards, editor states, logs, benchmarks, testimonials, or editorial text pacing

## Scene Posture Modifiers

These are not primary archetypes. Use them as scene-level modifiers when evidence supports them:

- **Cinematic Product Surface:** the UI fills 80-95% of frame, external chrome stays quiet, action-safe zones stay respected, motion uses hold-then-snap.
- **Proof-Led Prestige:** metrics, benchmarks, logs, or trust signals are grouped into controlled clusters; proof should feel boardroom-ready, not social-ad flashy.

## Rejection Test

If the generated video could be relabeled as another SaaS brand with only a logo swap, the archetype inference failed. Re-check `BrandTruth`, reduce borrowed signals, and restore product-specific evidence.
